# AI-default patterns

Independently written for this repository, informed by `madebymustafa/design-taste`, TasteSkill v2 and [Ayush Soni's design indicators](https://x.com/ayushsoni_io/status/2115322579187867898) (see `metadata.json`), and framed like `app-audit`'s AI-template patterns. Use these as questions in the distinctiveness check, not as a ban list. Judge their accumulation and purpose against the product, references and recorded direction; familiarity alone is not a defect.

## The first test

Could this screen have come from typing the page type ("landing page", "settings screen", "dashboard") into an agent with no brief, references, or `DESIGN.md`? If honestly yes, the direction has not reached the screen. Find which recorded decision is missing from it.

## Fingerprints to look for

| Pattern | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Template hero | Centred badge pill, oversized bold headline, two equal buttons, product screenshot in browser chrome. |
| Default palette | Indigo-to-purple or blue-to-purple gradient backgrounds or gradient text; Inter on slate as the whole palette; dark navy as automatic "premium". |
| Card trios | Three equal cards as the default section shape; identical symmetric sections stacked down the page; cards nested inside cards. |
| Decoration as depth | Glass blur everywhere, a shadow on every surface, the same large radius on every element. |
| Emoji icons | Emoji standing in for icons in features, navigation, or status. |
| One neutral sans | A single neutral sans at every level with no display contrast, and many sizes competing in one viewport. |
| Accent everywhere | The accent colour appears so often it no longer points at anything; more than two colours carry meaning. |
| Generic action copy | "Get started free" or "Submit" regardless of what the action does. |
| Removable sections | A section could be deleted without losing anything the page argues. |
| The "editorial fix" | The remedy for the first two waves, now its own default: cream or yellow-cream ground with a terracotta, rust, or clay accent; a small tracked uppercase label above every heading; a pill badge above the hero headline; one headline word in italic serif or colour; hairline rules and zero radius everywhere as newspaper cosplay; performative section labels ("Field notes", "Quietly trusted by"). |
| Decorative scaffolding | Tags, section numbers, rules, bordered containers or code-like breadcrumbs repeated for atmosphere rather than grouping or navigation. Does each help people orient, compare or act? Keep functional breadcrumbs and meaningful grouping. |
| Unnecessary detail | Elaborate interface mockups or diagrams, and layers of shades and opacity, that make a simple idea harder to read. Would less detail clarify the actual message without losing information, hierarchy or accessible contrast? Keep diagrams and detail that explain or support the task. |
| Formulaic type treatment | A fashionable display face, italic emphasis or tight letter spacing applied as a recipe instead of serving the content and identity. Is the choice legible and purposeful? An established face or intentional emphasis is valid. |
| Long display sentence | A full sentence set at display size over three or more lines, read as a long headline whatever its markup. |
| Invented proof | Placeholder logos, quotes, star rows, or customer counts. |

## Tells are dated

A pattern becomes a tell by being produced by default everywhere, not by being bad design; most began as good ideas on good sites. That is why this list never names a replacement font, colour, or component: every published "use this instead" becomes the next wave of tells. Replace a tell with a procedure (derive it from the subject, the references, and the recorded direction), not with a product. When a project's first renders keep producing a pattern, name it in that project's `DESIGN.md` anti-goals so later generations avoid it by name.

## Correcting one

Trace the pattern to the missing decision (content priority, the signature, a typography role, an anti-goal) and correct the screen from that decision. Do not swap one default for another fashionable default; the fix should make the screen more like this product, not more like a different template.

Removal is not enough. A page with every tell stripped out and nothing in their place is an empty page with good manners, which is its own tell. In TasteSkill's blind tests, a rich page full of familiar devices beat a clean page that was thin every time. Fill the gap with the idea, real content, and real pictures before running these checks.
