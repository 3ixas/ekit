# Product Demo

## Outcome

A product illustration or authored demo that communicates one useful idea at its intended size and fits the approved product direction.

## Method

1. Establish placement, audience, aspect ratio, delivery format, responsive sizes, product identity, permitted assets and the one action or result to communicate. Read the project’s approved design direction and inspect the actual product when available. If a consequential direction is unsettled, return it to `interface-design`. Separate known product behaviour from invented or proposed behaviour.
2. Plan the composition before building: focal object, supporting context, crop, content and, for motion, a short sequence from action to payoff. Keep the product recognisable; make essential text, touched controls and the result legible and whole. Crop or simplify supporting detail as appropriate. Redesign narrow layouts when scaling makes them illegible. These are choices, not fixed dimensions or a mandated visual style.
3. Choose the lightest suitable medium and project-compatible tools. Reuse the product’s components and tokens when they fit; a deliberately simplified illustration can be authored independently. Check rights and privacy before using logos, screenshots, real data or third-party assets. [Stageshow](https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills/tree/802057409787133703c56001080169e347305572/stageshow) is an optional reference or starting point, not a required dependency: inspect its asset notices, compatibility and licences before copying anything.
4. Build the still or poster first and inspect it in its destination at actual display sizes. For animation, make the action and result readable, keep transitions causally coherent, hold the payoff long enough to read and make any reset intelligible. Provide a meaningful reduced-motion or static presentation; use `motion` for choreography and implementation.
5. Inspect the rendered result in context at representative wide and narrow sizes. For motion, watch real playback and examine the opening, payoff, busiest intermediate state and loop seam; check the poster and reduced-motion presentation. Fix observed defects and repeat affected checks. Automated geometry or contact sheets assist review but cannot establish visual quality or real product functionality. Report the artefact, what was inspected and any missing evidence.

## Boundaries

A scripted cursor and simulated state are illustration, not evidence of the product working. Do not present invented capabilities, outcomes or metrics as verified claims. Use `verify` on the actual product when functional proof is requested. `interface-design` owns the wider visual direction; `motion` owns motion craft; `images` owns raster generation. A skill does not authorize publication, external disclosure, paid services or unlicensed assets.
