# Humanize

Use this mode to restore authorship and natural thought to prose that feels generic or machine-produced.

## Diagnose before changing words

Synthetic writing is usually a reasoning or voice problem before it is a vocabulary problem. Look for:

- claims that could belong to anyone;
- a personal history or motivation reduced to a generic professional summary;
- a sequence of credentials with no turning point, reason, or point of view;
- borrowed praise for the audience, organisation, or opportunity instead of a specific reason the writer fits this context;
- smooth transitions that hide a missing logical step;
- uniform paragraph and sentence shapes;
- excessive framing, summaries, headings, or symmetrical lists;
- abstract praise and inflated importance without evidence;
- canned contrasts, throat-clearing, fake questions, and repeated conclusions;
- confident details, feelings, or anecdotes the author never supplied.

## Repair

Recover the author’s actual point from their draft, notes, or conversation. Replace vague claims with supported specifics. Make the logical relationship explicit or remove the transition. Let emphasis and rhythm follow the thought. Preserve useful roughness, restraint, humour, idiom, and uncertainty when they belong to the author.

When a piece is meant to present a person, restore the causal arc that makes the account theirs: what changed, what they discovered, what they chose, and what they want now. Use the smallest number of concrete details that prove that arc. Make alignment specific to the reader or context without copying its language or inventing enthusiasm.

Remove a recognizable AI phrase only when it weakens the prose. A banned-word list alone produces different generic writing. Never add slang, fragments, typos, personal memories, or contrived opinions as a disguise.

## Check

Read the result for meaning drift. Confirm that every personal detail and factual claim came from the author or a cited source. The final prose should sound authored because it contains a real point of view expressed precisely, not because it performs informality.
