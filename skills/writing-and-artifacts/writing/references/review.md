# Review

Review diagnoses a piece and helps the user decide what to change. It does not assume permission to rewrite.

## Review lenses

Use only the lenses relevant to the writing:

- **Purpose:** Is the intended outcome clear and realistic?
- **Reader:** Does the piece meet the reader’s knowledge, objections, and incentives?
- **Argument:** Do conclusions follow from the evidence? Are counterarguments or qualifications missing?
- **Structure:** Does the order make the reasoning easy to follow?
- **Voice:** Does it sound consistent with the author and context?
- **Clarity:** Are important sentences precise on one reading?
- **Credibility:** Are facts, quotations, citations, and numerical claims verifiable?
- **Form:** Does it obey the useful conventions and constraints of its medium?

## Report findings

Lead with the overall judgment and the few changes that would most improve the outcome. For each material finding, point to the relevant passage, explain its effect on the reader, and propose a concrete direction. Separate factual errors from editorial preferences. Mention strengths only when they should be preserved during revision.

When source verification matters, check authoritative sources and distinguish what was verified, inferred, and still unknown. Do not present a style preference as a rule.
