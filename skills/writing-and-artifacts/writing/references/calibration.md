# Calibration

Use only when the author asks to learn from revisions or evaluate a voice-guidance change. Ordinary writing needs no corpus, interview, judge, or repeated drafting loop. Follow the personal-context loading and missing-file rules in `SKILL.md`.

## Learn from edits

1. With authorised material, pair each source and original draft with the author’s final revision. Compare the actual changes in context and, where available, the author’s explanation. Keep task-specific constraints and factual corrections separate from voice preferences.
2. Classify meaningful edits as voice, structure or format, factual fidelity, or wording. An edit may have more than one cause. Identify what was preserved as well as what changed.
3. Treat a single edit as a hypothesis, not a rule. Look across multiple pieces for a recurring pattern, or rely on explicit author feedback. Never infer a general ban from a word count or apply a stylistic habit mechanically.
4. Propose concise, evidence-backed preference changes with examples and the contexts where they apply. The author reviews them before any durable personal-profile update; do not silently update preferences or disclose private examples in a public repository.

## Evaluate a change

Keep some authorised source/final pairs held out from developing the proposed guidance. On the same held-out briefs and source facts, produce outputs using the current and proposed guidance under comparable conditions. Compare source fidelity, preserved qualifications and distinctive details, voice, usefulness for the reader, and the author’s editing effort. The author’s assessment decides whether the change is useful. Word-level edit distance may support comparison but is not a quality score: factual improvements, different valid phrasing and changes in intent can all affect it.

Record the evidence, sample limitations and unresolved differences without claiming a measured improvement from agreement with examples alone. If no suitable held-out pairs or author assessment are available, report that limitation rather than inventing a result. Keep private material in its authoritative private location; do not create a public corpus or send it to another service without authority.

For a substantial piece, an optional review can identify the few most consequential fixes before revision. Check factual fidelity against the source and preserve the author’s final control. Do not require a separate model, automated judge, fixed number of rounds, or an AI detector.
