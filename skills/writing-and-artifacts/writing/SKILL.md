# Writing

## Outcome

Usable prose that sounds like Elias or the intended author and does its actual job.

## Method

Name the piece's job as an activity the reader must be moved through (decide, approve, change a belief) rather than the artefact's type; choose the smallest develop, draft, edit, review, humanize, voice, or final route; preserve authorship and sources; structure before line polish; test the argument and intended reader; repair unsupported connective tissue before lexical AI patterns; use optional VOICE and project STYLE evidence when supplied; return the artifact first.

For personal or persuasive writing, first find the writer's throughline: the turning point, tension, reason, or point of view that makes the piece theirs. Let that throughline organise the draft. Use concrete actions, choices, and observations to show qualities instead of naming generic strengths. When the writing needs to establish fit or motivation, explain both sides of the connection: what the writer has actually done, why this particular context matters to them, and what the reader can reasonably expect them to contribute. Use the target's language only where the supplied evidence supports it; do not mirror a brief or praise an audience in place of making the writer distinctive.

Before returning a draft, run a distinctiveness pass. Ask whether the central point could belong to anyone, whether the most memorable details came from the source, whether each paragraph advances the reader's decision or understanding, and whether the prose sounds like a person with a view rather than a polished profile. Prefer one or two telling specifics over a catalogue of credentials. Stop when the piece has done its job.

On every invocation of this skill, before developing, drafting, editing, reviewing, humanizing or finalizing prose, read both personal context files in full: the current `voice-profile.md` named by the active personal instructions and its sibling `writing-preferences.md` in the same directory. This is required even for short requests, another author's prose or a project with its own style. Re-read both for each new invocation; summaries, remembered preferences and selected sections do not satisfy this step. If a tool truncates either file, retrieve the remaining content before proceeding. Do not open every external reference linked inside them.

Distinguish observed habits from chosen development preferences and apply the context appropriate to the task; reading both files does not require using every style or imposing Elias's voice on another author. Before returning the result, check it against both files and the current request. If either file cannot be located or read, explicitly identify the missing file and state that personal-context loading is incomplete; preserve the supplied draft's observable voice and do not claim a full voice match. Ordinary writing does not require a new preference interview.

Read only the route that fits the request:

- [Develop](references/develop.md) when the ideas, evidence, angle, or structure are not ready to draft.
- [Draft](references/draft.md) when creating a first complete version.
- [Edit](references/edit.md) when improving an existing draft while preserving its meaning and voice.
- [Review](references/review.md) when the user wants diagnosis, feedback, fact checking, or an editorial decision rather than an automatic rewrite.
- [Humanize](references/humanize.md) when prose feels generic, synthetic, over-smoothed, or unlike its author.
- [Technical writing](references/technical-writing.md) for documentation, explanations, proposals, incident writing, and engineering decisions.
- [Voice and style](references/voice-and-style.md) when establishing or applying a reusable author or project profile. Optional starting templates live in [VOICE.md](assets/VOICE.md) and [STYLE.md](assets/STYLE.md).

## Boundaries

Do not invent experiences, motives, feelings, or outcomes to make a piece more compelling. Do not turn a real story into a marketing pitch, imitate named writers, force onboarding, or treat drafting as sending or publishing authority. Never trade factual precision or the author's recognizable voice for a more impressive-sounding result.
