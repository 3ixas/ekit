# Product Thinking

## Outcome

A defensible verdict on one product idea before meaningful build time: **build**, **test first**, **reshape**, or **drop**. It names the riskiest assumption and the cheapest test that would settle it.

## Method

Load `grilling` and use its collaborative exploration throughout the product lenses, not just its question format. Help develop promising answers as well as challenge weak ones: explain why a lens matters here, make alternatives concrete, and use the user's reasoning to refine the idea. Attribute the principle briefly, then explain its practical consequence; a thinker's name alone does not make a question understandable. Keep uncertain demand and product claims as hypotheses until supported by evidence. Fold the brief reusable-thinking reflection into the existing verdict without adding a teaching phase or extending this skill beyond its verdict and authority boundaries.

1. **Frame.** Restate the idea in one sentence: who, what struggle, what changes for them. If the idea's home is known, read it: the project repository's docs, or the idea's vault note through `obsidian`. Read `10 Areas/Venture/Notes/Venture Idea Validation Framework.md` and related idea notes as one input among the lenses, not the lead. If the idea is too vague to restate in one sentence, run an options round instead: three framings, each betting on a different audience, struggle, or ambition, with one stretch framing. Continue with the framing Elias chooses.
2. **Question in rounds.** Use `grilling`'s round format, including its options rounds for framing and reshaping: number every question that can be asked now, and give a recommended answer or a likely weak point for each. Work the lenses in order, because a failure early makes later lenses moot:
   1. [Problem reality](references/lenses/problem-reality.md)
   2. [Demand and pull](references/lenses/demand-and-pull.md)
   3. [Differentiation](references/lenses/differentiation.md)
   4. [Scope and taste](references/lenses/scope-and-taste.md)
   5. [Distribution](references/lenses/distribution.md)
   6. [Strategy and moat](references/lenses/strategy-and-moat.md)

   Open a lens file when you reach that lens. Skip a lens that plainly does not apply, and say why (a personal utility needs no moat). Go deeper on the weakest lens.
3. **Push back.** Treat compliments, hypotheticals, and "people would love it" as missing evidence. Ask for the specific person, the last time it happened, and what they did about it. Name the lens and thinker behind each hard question in a few words, for example "(Fitzpatrick: past behaviour, not opinions)".
4. **Use cases as analogies, never proof.** When a company's decision resembles Elias's situation, open [company cases](references/company-cases.md) and ask whether their reason applies here. Always include the counter-lesson.
5. **Stop** when the riskiest assumption is named and further questions cannot sharpen it, or when a lens has clearly failed.
6. **Verdict.** Report:
   - the verdict and a one-line reason;
   - the riskiest assumption;
   - the cheapest test that would settle it, with the signal that counts as a pass;
   - the lenses that failed or stayed weak;
   - open questions.

   On a **reshape** verdict, follow it with an options round of three reshaped ideas, each changing a different element (audience, job, scope, or model), one of them a stretch. The chosen reshape starts a new pass from step 1.

   Then propose writing the verdict and any exploration trail to the idea's home. Write only after Elias approves.

## Attribution

Every named principle comes from the [roster](references/roster.md), which lists each thinker's primary sources. Paraphrase; never invent a quotation or claim a thinker holds a view the roster does not support. On request, answer "how would X push back?" as that thinker's recorded principle applied to the idea, framed as an application, not their words. Do not role-play anyone.

## Boundaries

- `ideate` generates options; `strategy` writes `STRATEGY.md`; `user-research` plans studies. After a **build** or **test first** verdict, `grill-with-docs` shapes the software scope.
- A verdict is judgment from the conversation's evidence, not market research. When a question needs current facts (competitors, pricing, regulation), use `research` rather than guessing.
- Never contact people, publish, or spend money to run a test; propose it.
