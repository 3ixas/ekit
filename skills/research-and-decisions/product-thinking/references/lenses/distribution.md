# Distribution

How will the first hundred users arrive, and why would they tell anyone?

## Questions

- Where exactly will the first ten users come from? Name the places and people. (Graham: founders recruit early users by hand.)
- How fast do new users reach the moment of value? (Bier: every tap is scarce; time-to-value decides whether a consumer product spreads.)
- What would make someone tell others, and is that built into the product or bolted on afterwards? (Chesky: an experience remarkable enough to talk about is what spreads; Bier: marketing and product growth are one funnel, not separate jobs.)
- Can the first market be narrow and dense enough to reach critical mass? (Graham: Facebook began with one university; Bier: saturate one community so adoption reinforces itself.)
- Does the channel match the price? A low price rarely supports paid sales; a high price rarely spreads by word of mouth alone. (Cagan: business viability includes customer acquisition cost and channel.)

## Failure signals

- "It will go viral" or "we'll post about it" is the whole plan.
- The first users cannot be named or reached.
- Time-to-value is long and nothing shortens it.

## Passing looks like

A named, reachable first cohort, a short path to value, and a plausible reason people will pass it on.
