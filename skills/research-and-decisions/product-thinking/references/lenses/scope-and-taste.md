# Scope and taste

What is the smallest version that proves the idea, and what quality bar must it meet?

## Questions

- What is the one experience that must be excellent, and what can be manual, missing, or faked? (Graham: do things that don't scale; recruit users by hand and serve them far beyond what scales.)
- Can a first version ship in weeks, not months? What would it take to launch and start getting feedback? (Seibel: launch fast; a landing page and a spreadsheet can be an MVP.)
- Does the market tolerate a rough first version, or is quality the entry ticket? (Seibel vs Artman: a novel need forgives roughness; a crowded one does not. Decide which applies.)
- Picture the 1-star, 5-star, and 11-star version of the key moment. What is the achievable sweet spot? (Chesky: extrapolate the experience to absurd extremes, then pull back.)
- Which features could be cut and the idea would still be itself? (Jobs, 1997: focus means saying no; Apple cut its line to four products.)
- Which risk is the MVP designed to retire: value, usability, feasibility, or business viability? (Cagan: test the four big risks before building, not after.)
- Is the builder the target user, and would they use it daily? (Saarinen: Linear was built by people who used it, with craft as a deliberate choice.)

## Failure signals

- The first version is the whole product.
- No one can say which risk the first version tests.
- Quality is treated as optional in a market where it is the entry ticket.

## Passing looks like

A first version scoped to one excellent moment that retires a named risk, with a realistic launch window.
