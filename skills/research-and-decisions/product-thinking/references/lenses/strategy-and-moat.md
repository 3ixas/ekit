# Strategy and moat

If it works, does it last, and is it the right thing to spend on?

## Questions

- How does it delight customers in ways that are hard to copy and that improve margin? (Biddle: the DHM model; an easy-to-copy delight is gone within weeks.)
- What does this cost you in things you will not do instead? (Doshi: judge work by opportunity cost, not just return.)
- Imagine it has failed a year from now. What were the most likely reasons? Which are real threats, which are unfounded worries, and which are the elephants nobody is naming? (Doshi: the pre-mortem.)
- Which decisions here are one-way doors, and which can be reversed cheaply? Decide the reversible ones quickly. (Bezos, 2015 letter: Type 1 and Type 2 decisions.)
- Is this riding a trend that is growing or fading? (Bezos, 2016 letter: embrace external trends; Graham: live in the future and build what's missing.)
- Is a metric, survey, or process standing in for the real customer outcome? (Bezos, 2016 letter: resist proxies.)

## Failure signals

- Success would be instantly copyable by a larger player, with nothing compounding.
- The pre-mortem names a likely failure the idea has no answer to.
- A one-way door is being treated as a quick experiment.

## Passing looks like

A source of advantage that compounds, a pre-mortem whose serious risks have mitigations, and an honest opportunity cost.

This lens matters most for ventures. For a personal tool or a learning project, ask only the opportunity-cost and pre-mortem questions.
