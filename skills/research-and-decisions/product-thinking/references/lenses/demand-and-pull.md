# Demand and pull

Will anyone want this badly enough to switch, pay, or put up with a rough first version?

## Questions

- Who wants this so urgently they would use a crude version built by an unknown maker? (Graham: a small number of people who want it a lot beats many who mildly like it.)
- What commitment have they given: time, reputation, a pre-payment, an introduction? (Fitzpatrick: commitment and advancement separate real interest from politeness.)
- What pulls them toward the new thing, and what anxiety about switching or habit of the present holds them back? (Moesta: the four forces of progress; switching happens only when push and pull outweigh anxiety and habit.)
- If they could no longer use it, how many would be very disappointed? Who, specifically? (Ellis: the 40% "very disappointed" benchmark, measured on real users.)
- Among the people who would be very disappointed, what do they have in common, and who is the high-expectation customer? (Vohra: segment by enthusiasm and build for that core.)
- Is there latent demand: behaviour people already show, clumsily, that the product would make effortless? (Bier: look for latent demand rather than creating new behaviour.)

## Failure signals

- Interest is broad and shallow; no one is urgent.
- Switching costs, anxiety, or habit clearly outweigh the gain, and the idea has no answer to them.
- The "very disappointed" group cannot be described.

## Passing looks like

A nameable core segment with urgent need, at least one form of commitment, and a credible reason the forces favour switching.
