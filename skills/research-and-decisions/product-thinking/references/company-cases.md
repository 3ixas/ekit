# Company cases

Analogies, never proof. Each case shows one decision and its principle, then the counter-lesson. The famous version of a story survives because it worked; many companies made the same move and failed. Use a case to ask "does their reason apply to you?", not "they did it, so you should".

## Apple, 1997: cutting the line

- **Decision:** Jobs returned to a sprawling product line and cut it to four products: consumer and pro, desktop and portable. (Isaacson, *Steve Jobs*, ch. 25)
- **Principle:** Focus means saying no to good options so a few things can be great.
- **Counter-lesson:** Apple could cut because it had an existing brand, customers, and distribution. A new product has nothing to cut; its version of focus is choosing one segment and one job.

## Stripe: the Collison installation

- **Decision:** The founders set new users up on the spot, on their own laptops, instead of sending a link. (Graham, *Do Things that Don't Scale*)
- **Principle:** Recruit early users by hand, and remove every step between interest and use.
- **Counter-lesson:** It worked because developer payments was a painful schlep with urgent demand. Hand-holding cannot create demand that does not exist.

## Airbnb: doing it by hand, then the 11-star experience

- **Decision:** The founders personally went to early hosts to improve listings, and later designed an extreme version of the guest journey to find what would make it remarkable. (Graham, *Do Things that Don't Scale*; Chesky, Masters of Scale)
- **Principle:** Serve the first users far beyond what scales, and learn what "remarkable" means before optimising.
- **Counter-lesson:** Manual effort teaches only if someone turns the lessons into the product. Without that it is just a cost.

## Superhuman: measuring product-market fit

- **Decision:** Surveyed users with the "very disappointed" question, segmented to the enthusiastic core, and split the roadmap between that core's loves and the near-fans' blockers. The score rose from 22% to 58%. (First Round Review)
- **Principle:** Product-market fit can be measured and steered, not just felt.
- **Counter-lesson:** It needs enough real users to survey. Before launch, the equivalent is commitment in customer conversations, not a survey of friends.

## Linear: quality as the entry ticket

- **Decision:** Entered a crowded issue-tracker market with a deliberately high-quality first version, invited users in waves, and asked for payment early. (Artman; Saarinen)
- **Principle:** When users already have good options, the first version must be substantially better for a narrow audience.
- **Counter-lesson:** The founders were expert users with design backgrounds and long industry experience. Quality-first costs time, and it only pays off when the team can actually deliver a better product.

## Quibi: scale before evidence

- **Decision:** Launched in April 2020 with large funding and premium short-form shows for phones. It shut down six months later. Its founders' open letter said the idea may not have been strong enough for a standalone service, or the timing was wrong, and probably both. ([CNBC, Oct 2020](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/10/21/quibi-to-shut-down-after-just-6-months.html))
- **Principle:** Money and talent do not substitute for evidence of demand. The riskiest assumption was never tested cheaply.
- **Counter-lesson:** Launching into a pandemic was genuine bad luck. Don't treat every failure as proof of a single cause.
